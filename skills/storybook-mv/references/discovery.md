# Adaptive discovery and convergence

## Begin with evidence and intent

Identify whether the user is exploring, choosing a direction, requesting a proposal, asking for production or revising a result. Extract already known facts. Analyze actual references: composition, shape, texture, color, spatial layers, character/type behavior, editing and the role of lyrics. Read a sequence to judge motion. Do not call an unseen clip's acting bad or infer a production method from appearance alone.

In an early exchange, clarify the few factors that change the route most: what the viewer should feel/understand, what the user wants to retain from the reference, what failed previously, and major asset/time constraints. Ask the song or source directly when it is unknown.

Song/length/aspect/style answers alone never finish discovery. Continue with what the film actually shows, its subject/core idea, concrete development and ending/loop. For lyrical songs read the actual lyrics, distinguish supported readings from artist facts, and let the user choose material interpretive forks. Do not silently invent a romance or map vague emotion words into a full film.

## Ask useful small rounds

Use 1–3 questions, normally 2. Choose the next round from missing information, not a fixed questionnaire. Offer 2–4 concrete choices and free text. Keep each question to one interpretable dimension.

Examples of useful dimensions:
- Calm, lingering shots versus frequent information/action changes.
- Recognizable human features versus a symbolic or abstract protagonist.
- What a character needs to visibly do: glance, walk, handle objects, dance, sing.
- Whether all-code is mandatory, provided art is allowed, or generated footage is acceptable.
- Every lyric line, selected words, environmental text, or no text.
- Whole song versus a selected section; landscape/portrait based on intended viewing.

Do not ask all dimensions when they are irrelevant. Do not bundle “short portrait cheap” and “long landscape expensive” as if those were one preference.

Avoid empty labels such as “high-end” or “cinematic” without describing their visual behavior. When the user selects everything, ask a concrete priority or show a tradeoff. Do not ask which model/renderer they prefer if an artistic question and a professional recommendation would resolve the choice.

Keep a missing song/version question visible in the actual answer flow. Do not repeatedly place it outside a widget whose answers are being returned alone.

## Compare viable directions

After understanding the goal/constraints, offer 2–3 distinct directions when helpful, not an exhaustive style catalog. Explain the medium, shape language, movement and a representative moment for each, plus the actual implementation burden.

If visuals help, compare the same characters/scene/action across directions. Honor the asset policy for preview creation. A generated concept picture is not evidence that a coding renderer can reproduce the look or animate it.

Preserve choices in brief/decisions. Unlock a choice only when the user changes it or new feasibility evidence requires discussion. Keep the user's language in concise notes without hoarding the whole chat.

## Know when to stop

A proposal is ready when the desired experience, visual language, content progression, output scope and asset constraints are concrete enough to describe representative moments and a plausible production route. Identify remaining nonblocking assumptions.

Do not continue questioning about every color/font/prop. Choose routine details as proposed implementation decisions. Do not produce the final execution prescription while major style/content constraints are unknown.

Recap the concrete content and selected song reading, then wait for user acceptance when it is missing. Do not start asset creation, a pilot or full rendering while asking that question. Reuse genuine earlier accepted content and scope; generic production intent is not content acceptance. An approved short study cannot expand to a full song. Follow the hard content/song gates linked directly from SKILL.md.
